## 2. Project Objective

### What ReportSaathi Will Accomplish
ReportSaathi will serve as a plain-language health literacy companion that converts uploaded medical laboratory reports and printed prescriptions into clear, structured explanations in the user's preferred language, paired with tailored questions for their next doctor consultation.

### Target MVP Audience
* Everyday patients in India receiving diagnostic lab reports or printed prescriptions from independent local laboratories, small clinics, or hospitals.
* Family caregivers translating and explaining medical documents for elderly, non-English-speaking, or low-health-literacy relatives.

### Core Problem Solved
Patients frequently receive English-only medical documents dense with clinical jargon, unfamiliar units, and prescription shorthand (e.g., BD, TDS, HS) during rushed 5–10 minute consultations. Existing alternatives either provide raw unassisted PDFs (diagnostic apps), exclude local clinics (hospital portals), cost too much to scale (tele-helplines), or introduce mistyping, hallucinated reference ranges, and panic (general AI chatbots and internet searches).

### Value Provided
* **Document-Anchored Clarity:** Eliminates manual typing errors via OCR and evaluates lab results strictly against the reference ranges printed on the user's actual report.
* **Accessible Language & Depth:** Delivers explanations in plain regional languages at user-selectable reading depths.
* **Consultation Readiness:** Generates 3–5 focused, non-directive questions to help patients communicate effectively with their physician.
* **Ephemeral Privacy:** Processes documents without requiring account creation or permanent storage of sensitive medical records.

### What ReportSaathi Will Deliberately Avoid Doing
* Diagnosing diseases, naming probable conditions, or interpreting clinical syndromes.
* Recommending treatments, lifestyle regimens, or over-the-counter remedies.
* Advising users to start, stop, skip, or modify prescribed medication dosages or schedules.
* Inventing, inferring, or substituting missing laboratory reference ranges from external databases.
* Operating as an emergency triage service or clinical decision-support tool for healthcare providers.

## 3. Core User Journey

The end-to-end user flow below distinguishes mandatory MVP capabilities from post-MVP enhancements.

| Step | User Action / System Behavior | Release Scope | Notes & Constraints |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Opening ReportSaathi** | User accesses the application via mobile or desktop browser without mandatory login. | **MVP** | Must display clear educational purpose and privacy notice upfront. |
| **2. Selecting Preferred Language** | User chooses output language (English, Hindi, or Kannada). | **MVP** | Selection persists across the active session. |
| **3. Selecting Explanation Level** | User selects comprehension depth (`Simple`, `Standard`, or `Detailed`). | **MVP** | Defaults to `Simple` or `Standard` (UI default pending confirmation). |
| **4. Uploading Medical Document** | User uploads a lab report or printed prescription (`PDF`, `JPG`, `JPEG`, `PNG` up to 10 MB) and provides explicit processing consent. | **MVP** | Must block unsupported file types and files exceeding size limits before upload. |
| **5. Extracting Information (OCR)** | System extracts test names, numeric values, units, printed reference ranges, or printed medication instructions. | **MVP** | Handwritten prescriptions excluded from automated extraction in MVP. |
| **6. Reviewing & Correcting Extracted Data** | User views extracted fields side-by-side with the uploaded document and edits any OCR errors before generation. | **MVP** | Mandatory verification checkpoint for low-confidence OCR fields. |
| **7. Explaining Terminology & Values** | System generates plain-language definitions of each test/medication in the chosen language and depth. | **MVP** | Strictly preserves original test names, numbers, units, and dosage text. |
| **8. Displaying Reference-Range Status** | System labels lab values (`Within Printed Range`, `Below Printed Range`, `Above Printed Range`, or `Range Not Printed`). | **MVP** | Computed deterministically against printed report ranges only. |
| **9. Showing Medical Safety Warnings** | System displays standard medical disclaimers and deterministic escalation banners if urgent/red-flag thresholds are triggered. | **MVP** | Rule-based backend checks execute independently of LLM output. |
| **10. Generating Doctor Questions** | System produces 3–5 neutral, actionable questions tailored to out-of-range values or prescription instructions. | **MVP** | Phrased to support doctor-patient dialogue without suggesting diagnoses. |
| **11. Saving, Sharing, or Downloading** | User downloads a formatted plain-language summary PDF or shares via native device share/ephemeral link. | **MVP (PDF Download)** / **Pending (Link Sharing)** | Local PDF download is MVP; server-hosted share links require security/retention review. |
| **12. Providing Optional Feedback** | User submits optional helpfulness rating or flags an unclear/inaccurate explanation. | **MVP** | Must not capture or log underlying PHI alongside feedback metadata. |
| **13. Multi-Report Trend Tracking** | User saves longitudinal lab history across visits to view trends over time. | **Later** | Excluded from MVP to avoid permanent PHI storage and authentication overhead. |
| **14. Voice / Audio Read-Aloud** | System reads explanations aloud in regional languages for non-reading users. | **Later** | High accessibility value; postponed until text translation quality is validated. |

## 6. Medical Safety Requirements

The following non-negotiable safety rules apply to all MVP components:

1. **No Disease Diagnosis:** System outputs must never state, suggest, or rank potential diseases or medical conditions (e.g., never state *"This indicates diabetes"* or *"You may have anemia"*).
2. **No Treatment Recommendations:** Outputs must not recommend medications, supplements, dietary changes, or home remedies.
3. **No Medication Modifications:** Outputs must never instruct or suggest starting, stopping, skipping, or altering the dose, frequency, or duration of any prescribed drug.
4. **No Invented Reference Ranges:** Laboratory status indicators may only be computed when a numeric value and a corresponding reference range are explicitly present on the uploaded document. Missing ranges must be labeled *"Reference range not printed on report."*
5. **Preservation of Original Values and Units:** Every explained lab test or medication must display the exact extracted test name, numeric value, unit (e.g., `mg/dL`, `mmol/L`), printed reference range, and original dosage instruction verbatim alongside the plain-language explanation.
6. **Clear Communication of Uncertainty:** If OCR confidence is low, text is illegible, or a medical term/abbreviation is ambiguous, the system must flag uncertainty explicitly rather than guessing.
7. **Mandatory User Verification of Uncertain OCR Results:** Users must review extracted values—and explicitly confirm or edit any low-confidence fields—before the system generates an explanation.
8. **Persistent Medical Disclaimers:** Every screen displaying explanations and every exported PDF must include a prominent disclaimer in the user's selected language stating that ReportSaathi is an educational comprehension tool, not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
9. **Deterministic Safety Checks Independent of LLMs:** A deterministic backend rule layer must scan extracted text/values for critical red-flag indicators and enforce disclaimer/escalation injection regardless of LLM generation behavior.
10. **Escalation Guidance for Potentially Urgent Findings:** When deterministic rules detect values flagged as critical on the report or known urgent markers, the system must display a clear, calm escalation banner advising prompt consultation with a qualified doctor or emergency medical service.
11. **Qualified Medical Professional Review Before Release:** All prompt templates, deterministic red-flag rules, shorthand expansion dictionaries, and regional language safety disclaimers must be reviewed and signed off by a qualified medical professional prior to production release.

## 7. Privacy and Legal Boundaries

Based on `02-research.md` and Indian regulatory frameworks, legal obligations are separated into confirmed baseline design requirements and items requiring formal legal review.

### Confirmed Product & Engineering Requirements
* **DPDP Act, 2023 (In Force — Sections 5, 6, 8, 12, 13):**
  * Provide a clear, standalone privacy notice in plain English and supported regional languages before document upload.
  * Obtain free, specific, informed, and unambiguous user consent prior to processing health documents.
  * Implement reasonable security safeguards (TLS encryption in transit, encrypted ephemeral processing) to prevent data breaches.
  * Provide an accessible grievance redressal contact mechanism.
* **IT Act, 2000 & SPDI Rules, 2011 (In Force):**
  * Treat uploaded medical reports and prescriptions as Sensitive Personal Data or Information (SPDI).
  * Maintain a published privacy policy and prohibit secondary use, sale, or unauthorized third-party sharing of health data.
* **CDSCO Medical Device Software (SaMD) Boundary (In Force):**
  * Maintain strict non-diagnostic, educational-only functionality so ReportSaathi does not cross into regulated Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) territory.
* **Third-Party AI API Terms (In Force):**
  * Use paid/enterprise API tiers (e.g., Gemini API Paid Services with Cloud Billing) configured to prohibit prompt/output logging for human review or model training.

### Issues Requiring Formal Legal Review (Not Yet Confirmed)
* **DPDP Subordinate Rules Applicability:** Confirm exact operational requirements once subordinate rules (e.g., breach notification timelines, consent artifacts) are fully enforced.
* **Consumer Protection Act, 2019 Liability Exposure:** Legal review of disclaimer wording, terms of use, and limitation-of-liability clauses regarding misinterpretation risks or OCR errors.
* **CDSCO Classification Confirmation:** Formal regulatory opinion confirming that deterministic out-of-range flagging and red-flag escalation banners do not trigger Class A/B SaMD classification.
* **ICMR AI Guidelines (2023) Alignment:** Determine applicable ethical validation and documentation standards for consumer-facing health literacy tools in India.

> **Compliance Note:** Implementing encryption, ephemeral deletion, and consent banners establishes technical safeguards, **not** legal compliance. Full compliance cannot be claimed until legal counsel completes regulatory review.

## 8. Data and Storage Scope

### Information Processed During Active Session
* Uploaded document binary (`PDF`, `JPG`, `JPEG`, `PNG`).
* Extracted OCR text and structured key-value pairs (test names, values, units, printed reference ranges, printed medication text).
* User-corrected field values and session preferences (selected language and explanation level).
* Generated plain-language explanations, safety banners, and doctor consultation questions.

### Temporary Storage vs. Permanent History
* **Temporary Processing Only (MVP):** Documents and extracted medical data exist solely for the duration of the active processing session to enable OCR review, explanation generation, and PDF export.
* **No Permanent Report History in MVP:** Storing historical medical reports requires user authentication, long-term PHI encryption at rest, breach monitoring, and formal DPDP erasure workflows. Permanent report history is **excluded from MVP**.

### Document Deletion & Log Protection
* **Deletion Mechanism:** Uploaded files and extracted health text must be purged from application memory and temporary storage immediately upon session completion, PDF download, or session timeout.
* **Log & Third-Party Protection:**
  * Application logs, crash reports, and analytics tools must **never** record raw document files, OCR text, patient names, contact details, or generated medical explanations.
  * Third-party OCR and LLM API calls must be configured with zero-data-retention / no-training settings and stripped of personally identifiable headers (such as patient name, age, phone number, or hospital ID) before transmission where technically feasible.
* **Storage Solution & Pending Policies:**
  * **Supabase** is under consideration as a proposed backend/storage option if ephemeral temporary object storage or anonymized feedback logging is needed, but **is not a confirmed technical decision**.
  * **Exact session retention timeout** (e.g., immediate post-request purge vs. short-lived TTL in minutes) and **cloud infrastructure backup deletion policies** are **pending confirmation**.

## 11. Final Scope Recommendation

### What the Team Should Build First
1. **Core Ephemeral Pipeline:** No-login responsive web interface with DPDP-aligned consent gate and file upload (`PDF`, `JPG`, `JPEG`, `PNG` up to `10 MB`).
2. **Human-in-the-Loop OCR & Deterministic Range Engine:** Printed lab report OCR extraction paired with a mandatory side-by-side user review/edit step and deterministic reference-range comparison.
3. **Safe Multilingual Explainer & PDF Export:** Controlled plain-language explanation across `English`, `Hindi`, and `Kannada` (`Simple`, `Standard`, `Detailed`), deterministic red-flag escalation banners, 3–5 doctor questions, and downloadable summary PDF.

### What Should Be Postponed
* **Handwritten Prescription Interpretation:** Postpone automated OCR for handwritten prescriptions until specialized handwriting models achieve validated accuracy; limit MVP prescription support to printed/typed prescriptions.
* **User Accounts & Longitudinal Report Storage:** Postpone until post-MVP validation justifies the security, authentication, and DPDP compliance overhead.
* **Additional Regional Languages & Audio Read-Aloud:** Postpone until clinical translation accuracy and safety guardrails are proven in Hindi and Kannada.
* **Server-Hosted Shareable Links:** Postpone in favor of local PDF download and native device file sharing to preserve strict zero-retention privacy.

### What Should Never Be Implemented
* Disease diagnosis, condition likelihood ranking, or symptom-checker chat.
* Treatment, diet, or home-remedy recommendations.
* Instructions to start, stop, or alter prescribed medication dosages.
* Substitution or invention of missing laboratory reference ranges.

### Most Important Risks to Resolve Before Development
1. **Medical Reviewer Onboarding & Red-Flag Rule Definition:** Establish clinical review ownership early so safety guardrails, shorthand expansions, and escalation rules are validated before coding prompts.
2. **OCR & LLM Vendor Selection with Zero-Retention Guarantees:** Lock in providers with signed/contractual no-training and data-retention controls (e.g., paid enterprise API tiers) and verify Hindi/Kannada script rendering in PDF exports.
3. **Pre-Processing PII Scrubbing & Ephemeral Architecture:** Finalize how patient identifiers (names, phone numbers) are stripped or isolated and confirm exact temporary memory/storage purge rules prior to implementation.
