# ReportSaathi – Problem Definition

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Product Name:** ReportSaathi (Plain-Language Medical Report and Prescription Explainer)  
**Role:** Senior Product Strategy  

---

## 1. Project Overview

**ReportSaathi** (meaning *"Report Companion"*) is a healthcare communication and patient empowerment tool designed to bridge the comprehension gap between medical documentation and everyday patients. 

When patients visit clinics or hospitals, they routinely receive complex laboratory reports, handwritten or typed prescriptions, and dense discharge summaries. ReportSaathi translates this clinical information into clear, plain language in the patient's preferred language. By strictly anchoring explanations to the printed reference ranges on the user's specific report and explaining prescribed medications without offering medical advice, ReportSaathi helps patients understand their health status, follow their treatment plans safely, and prepare informed questions for their next doctor's visit.

---

## 2. Target Users

*   **Primary Users: Patients Managing Acute or Chronic Conditions**
    *   Individuals undergoing routine blood work, managing chronic illnesses (e.g., diabetes, thyroid disorders, hypertension), or recovering after hospital discharge.
    *   *Example:* A 45-year-old teacher who receives a comprehensive metabolic panel via WhatsApp from a diagnostic lab and wants to know what "HbA1c: 6.8%" means before her follow-up appointment.
*   **Secondary Users: Family Caregivers**
    *   Adult children, spouses, or guardians managing healthcare logistics and medications for elderly parents or young children.
    *   *Example:* A working professional managing four different prescriptions and a hospital discharge summary for an aging parent who only reads Hindi or Tamil.
*   **Tertiary Beneficiaries: Healthcare Providers (Doctors & Nurses)**
    *   Clinicians who benefit from better-informed patients who ask structured, relevant questions and adhere more closely to prescribed regimens.

---

## 3. Main User Problems

1.  **Unfamiliar Clinical Jargon and Abbreviations:** Lab reports use technical terms (*"leukocytosis,"* *"bilateral,"* *"creatinine"*) and prescriptions use Latin shorthand (*"BD,"* *"TDS,"* *"HS,"* *"SOS"*), leaving patients confused about basic instructions.
2.  **Difficulty Interpreting Lab Values:** Patients see numbers, units (mg/dL, mmol/L), and flags (H/L) without understanding what the biomarker measures or how their number compares to the lab's printed normal range.
3.  **Language Barriers:** Most medical documents are printed in English, whereas millions of patients and caregivers think, speak, and feel most comfortable reading in their regional or native languages.
4.  **Consultation Time Constraints:** Doctors typically have only 5 to 10 minutes per patient. Patients often feel rushed, intimidated, or forget what to ask until after they have left the clinic.
5.  **Medication Confusion:** Patients frequently do not understand *why* a specific medicine was prescribed, whether it must be taken with food, or what common side effects to watch out for.

---

## 4. Existing Difficulties and Their Impact

Currently, when patients do not understand their medical documents, they resort to flawed workarounds that carry real-world consequences:

*   **Unverified Internet Searches:** Googling a single abnormal lab value often leads to worst-case scenarios (e.g., assuming mild fatigue and a slightly low hemoglobin count means a life-threatening disease), causing severe anxiety and panic.
*   **Unsafe Self-Medication or Non-Adherence:** Misreading prescription shorthand (e.g., confusing *OD* [once daily] with *BD* [twice daily]) or stopping a course of antibiotics early because they don't understand its purpose leads to treatment failure or adverse drug reactions.
*   **Delayed Care for Critical Values:** Patients may ignore genuinely dangerous lab results (such as critically high potassium or severely low platelet counts) simply because they do not recognize the urgency hidden behind technical terms.
*   **Repeated, Unproductive Clinic Visits:** Patients book extra appointments solely to have a doctor read a normal report aloud, straining an already overburdened healthcare system.

---

## 5. Proposed Solution

ReportSaathi acts as a safe, plain-language interpreter for medical documents. Users upload a photo, scan, or PDF of their lab report, prescription, or discharge summary, and select their preferred language.

### Core Capabilities & Strict Safety Guardrails

| What ReportSaathi DOES | What ReportSaathi NEVER DOES (Safety Boundaries) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Translates Jargon:** Explains medical terms and prescription abbreviations (e.g., *BID* = twice a day) in simple words. | **Never Diagnoses:** Never states or implies that a user has a specific disease or medical condition. |
| **Uses Printed Reference Ranges:** Compares the user's result *only* against the exact reference range printed on that specific lab's report. | **Never Invents Ranges:** Never pulls external or generic reference ranges if a range is missing or illegible on the document. |
| **Explains Prescribed Medicines:** Describes the general purpose of a prescribed drug (e.g., *"used to help lower blood pressure"*) and standard precautions. | **Never Prescribes or Alters Doses:** Never recommends new medicines, substitutes, or changes to the doctor's written dosage. |
| **Flags Urgent Warning Signs:** Highlights clearly abnormal/critical values or severe red-flag symptoms and advises prompt medical attention. | **Never Replaces a Doctor:** Explicitly reminds users in every interaction that explanations are educational and require clinical review. |
| **Generates Doctor Questions:** Provides 3–5 tailored, plain-language questions the patient can ask their physician during their visit. | **Never Stores Unnecessary PII:** Protects patient privacy by minimizing data retention and stripping personal identifiers where possible. |

## 6. Three Alternative Solution Approaches

Before committing to ReportSaathi's document-centric approach, we evaluated three alternative ways to solve this problem:

### Approach A: General Healthcare AI Chatbot (Open-Ended Q&A)
*   **How it works:** A conversational chatbot where patients type their symptoms or manually type out their lab results to ask health questions.
*   **Pros:** Flexible; no need to build optical character recognition (OCR) or document parsing pipelines.
*   **Cons:** High risk of users mistyping numbers or units; open-ended prompting easily drifts into unsafe territory (symptom checking and informal diagnosing); high friction for elderly or low-literacy users.

### Approach B: Clinic-Integrated EHR Patient Portal
*   **How it works:** Software sold directly to hospitals and diagnostic chains that automatically attaches plain-language explanations to reports generated inside their Electronic Health Record (EHR) system.
*   **Pros:** Clean, structured data (no messy handwriting or blurry photos); institutional trust.
*   **Cons:** Extremely slow B2B sales cycles; excludes patients who visit independent clinics, small local labs, or receive handwritten prescriptions.

### Approach C: Tele-Nurse / Pharmacist On-Demand Helpline
*   **How it works:** A service connecting patients via voice or chat with licensed nurses or pharmacists who manually review uploaded reports and explain them.
*   **Pros:** Human empathy, nuanced judgment, and high clinical safety.
*   **Cons:** High operational cost per query; difficult to scale across dozens of regional languages 24/7; unaffordable for low-income patients if charged per session.

---

## 7. Why ReportSaathi is a Useful Solution

ReportSaathi combines the accessibility of a consumer mobile tool with strict clinical guardrails, avoiding the pitfalls of the alternatives above:

1.  **Meets Patients Where They Are:** Works directly with the physical paper slips, PDFs, and WhatsApp images patients already receive—requiring zero integration from doctors or labs.
2.  **Grounded in the User's Actual Document:** Unlike open-ended chatbots that hallucinate or guess, ReportSaathi anchors every explanation strictly to the text and reference ranges printed on the uploaded document.
3.  **Linguistic Inclusion:** Empowers patients and caregivers in their native language, removing the barrier where English literacy dictates healthcare comprehension.
4.  **Transforms Passive Patients into Active Participants:** By generating concrete, relevant questions for the doctor, ReportSaathi makes short 5-minute consultations dramatically more productive for both patient and clinician.

---

## 8. Five Riskiest Assumptions

For ReportSaathi to succeed safely, the following five assumptions must hold true:

1.  **Document Readability (OCR Accuracy):** Users will upload photos of printed reports and handwritten prescriptions that are clear enough for our system to extract text and numbers without misreading decimal points (e.g., reading `1.5` as `15`).
2.  **Multilingual Medical Nuance:** Complex clinical concepts can be translated into regional languages accurately without losing meaning or accidentally sounding like medical advice.
3.  **Strict Guardrail Adherence:** The underlying AI system will consistently obey negative constraints (never diagnosing, never suggesting dose changes) even when users actively push or trick it into giving medical opinions.
4.  **User Comprehension & Action:** Receiving a plain-language summary and a list of questions will genuinely help patients feel calmer and communicate better with their doctors, rather than causing false reassurance.
5.  **Trust Without Over-Reliance:** Users will trust ReportSaathi enough to use it, while still respecting its boundaries and consulting a qualified doctor for actual medical decisions.

---

## 9. Medical Safety Risks

Because healthcare misinformation can cause physical harm, we must actively mitigate these primary clinical risks:

*   **Misreading Numbers or Units (OCR Errors):** Misinterpreting a decimal point, unit (e.g., `mcg` vs. `mg`), or handwritten drug name could lead to completely wrong explanations.
    *   *Mitigation:* Require user confirmation of extracted values/medicine names, and refuse to interpret blurry or ambiguous text (prompting the user to re-upload or ask their pharmacist).
*   **False Reassurance on Normal Lab Results:** A patient might have normal blood test numbers while experiencing severe physical symptoms (like chest pain), leading them to delay emergency care.
    *   *Mitigation:* Include prominent disclaimers that normal lab values do not rule out illness, and always screen for/highlight urgent red-flag symptoms.
*   **Missing Critical/Panic Values:** Failing to clearly distinguish a mildly elevated value from a life-threatening critical value.
    *   *Mitigation:* Use clear visual hierarchy and plain-language urgency tiers to advise immediate medical care when values fall far outside printed ranges.
*   **Implicit Diagnosis:** Explaining what a high marker *can* be associated with in a way that convinces the user they have a specific disease.
    *   *Mitigation:* Frame explanations around the biological function of the test (e.g., *"Creatinine is a waste product filtered by the kidneys"*) rather than listing scary diseases.

---

## 10. Privacy and Legal Risks

Medical documents contain some of the most sensitive personal data a person owns.

*   **Exposure of Protected Health Information (PHI):** Uploaded documents frequently display the patient's full name, age, phone number, address, doctor's name, and sensitive diagnoses (e.g., HIV, oncology, mental health).
    *   *Mitigation:* Automatically redact/mask personal identifiers before processing where possible, encrypt all data in transit and at rest, and auto-delete uploaded images immediately after generating the session summary.
*   **Regulatory & Legal Liability (Unauthorized Practice of Medicine):** If the product is perceived as providing medical diagnoses or treatment recommendations, it may violate healthcare regulations (such as India's Digital Personal Data Protection [DPDP] Act, Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, or HIPAA/FDA SaMD rules in the US).
    *   *Mitigation:* Maintain strict positioning as an *educational and administrative comprehension tool*, enforce hard-coded clinical guardrails, and display clear terms of use.
*   **Third-Party AI Data Retention:** Sending raw patient documents to external AI APIs that log data or use inputs to train future models.
    *   *Mitigation:* Use enterprise zero-data-retention agreements with AI providers so patient data is never stored or used for model training.

---

## 11. Project Success Criteria

We will measure ReportSaathi's success across safety, comprehension, and user empowerment:

1.  **Zero Critical Safety Violations:** 0% tolerance for outputs that diagnose diseases, recommend dosage changes, or invent reference ranges during red-team testing and clinical audits.
2.  **High Extraction & Explanation Accuracy:** $\ge 98\%$ accuracy in extracting numbers, units, and printed reference ranges from clear printed reports, verified by clinical reviewers.
3.  **Verified User Comprehension:** $\ge 85\%$ of surveyed users report clearly understanding what their lab values and prescription instructions mean after using ReportSaathi.
4.  **Consultation Empowerment:** $\ge 70\%$ of users report using at least one ReportSaathi-generated question during their follow-up doctor visit.
5.  **Privacy Compliance:** 100% of uploaded document images purged automatically within the defined ephemeral retention window (e.g., under 15 minutes).

---

## 12. What is Outside the Project Scope

To keep ReportSaathi focused, safe, and feasible to build, the following features are explicitly **out of scope**:

*   **Symptom Checker or Triage Engine:** ReportSaathi will not ask users about their symptoms to guess potential illnesses.
*   **Telemedicine or Doctor Marketplace:** We are not building video consultations, doctor booking, or live chat with physicians.
*   **E-Pharmacy or Medicine Ordering:** Users cannot purchase or order medicines through the platform.
*   **Long-Term Electronic Health Record (EHR) Storage:** ReportSaathi is a point-in-time comprehension tool, not a permanent cloud storage vault for lifelong medical history.
*   **Dietary Plans or Home Remedies:** We will not generate custom meal plans, supplements, or alternative medicine suggestions based on lab results.
*   **Interpreting Unprinted/Missing Reference Ranges:** If a lab report omits a reference range for a test, ReportSaathi will state that the range is not printed on the document rather than supplying one from external databases.
